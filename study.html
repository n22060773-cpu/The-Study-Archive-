How To Study

Studying is the process of learning, reviewing and understanding information so that it can be remembered and applied. Different students may learn and revise in different ways.

The Study Archive provides six study methods that can be used for different subjects and situations. Click on each method below to learn what it is, how to use it and when it may be useful.

Study Methods

Active Recall

What is it?
Active recall is a study method where you try to remember information without looking at your notes or textbook.

How can I use it?
Read or learn a topic first, then close your notes and try to write down or say everything you remember. You can also use flashcards or practice questions.

When is it useful?
It is useful when preparing for tests and exams because it helps you practise retrieving information from memory.

For further information: Birmingham City University - Active Recall

Spaced Repetition

What is it?
Spaced repetition involves reviewing information several times over increasing periods of time instead of studying everything in one session.

How can I use it?
Review a topic shortly after learning it, then review it again after a few days and continue reviewing it over time. Flashcards can be useful for this method.

When is it useful?
It is especially useful for remembering information over a longer period of time, such as vocabulary, definitions and key facts.

For further information: The Learning Scientists - Spaced Practice

Mind Mapping

What is it?
Mind mapping is a visual method of organising information around a central topic using connected ideas and keywords.

How can I use it?
Start with the main topic in the centre of a page. Add branches for the main ideas and smaller branches for supporting information.

When is it useful?
It can be useful when organising large topics, connecting ideas and reviewing information visually.

For further information: Grammarly - Mind Mapping

Pomodoro Technique

What is it?
The Pomodoro Technique is a time-management method where study is divided into focused periods followed by short breaks.

How can I use it?
Choose a task, work on it for a focused period of time, take a short break and then repeat the process. Longer breaks can be taken after several study periods.

When is it useful?
It can be useful when you have a large amount of work to complete or find it difficult to stay focused for a long period.

For further information: Todoist - Pomodoro Technique

SQ3R

What is it?
SQ3R is a reading and study method based on five steps: Survey, Question, Read, Recite and Review.

How can I use it?
First survey the material, create questions about it, read the information carefully, explain or recall what you have learned and finally review the material.

When is it useful?
SQ3R can be useful when studying textbooks, articles or other large amounts of written information.

For further information: Northern Arizona University - SQ3R Reading Method

Feynman Technique

What is it?
The Feynman Technique involves explaining a topic in simple language to check whether you actually understand it.

How can I use it?
Choose a topic and explain it as if you were teaching it to someone who does not know anything about it. Identify any parts that you cannot explain clearly and review them.

When is it useful?
It can be useful for checking your understanding of difficult concepts, especially in subjects where you need to explain processes or ideas.

For further information: Farnam Street - The Feynman Technique
